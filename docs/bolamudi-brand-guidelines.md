# Bolamudi Enterprise — Brand Guidelines

**Version 1.0 · August 2026**
Renewable Energy Project Development Support · Karnataka, India

---

## 0. How to read this document

Every rule here has a *why*. If you disagree with the why, change the rule — but change it deliberately, not by drift. A brand system dies from a hundred small unexplained exceptions, not from one big wrong decision.

Three things are locked in before anything else, because everything downstream depends on them:

| Decision | Choice | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning | Karnataka/India-forward regional authority | Palette is earth-derived, not corporate-navy. Photography is location-specific. Kannada is a first-class language. |
| Category signal | Established engineering consultancy, not startup | Small border radii, restrained motion, serif headings, no gradients, no emoji, no illustration of people. |
| Primary conversion asset | Credibility, not features | Layout gives more space to evidence (projects, MW, districts, approvals) than to marketing copy. |

**Critical open item:** the existing logo ("Option 2") has been audited in Section 1 and **fails 6 of 7 mandatory tests**. It is the single largest risk to this brand. Section 1.0 states the verdict and the reasoning; Section 1.2 gives the replacement direction. Every other section of this document assumes the logo is resolved along those lines.

## 1. Logo

### 1.0 Audit verdict on the existing mark ("Option 2")

**Result: 6 of 7 mandatory tests failed. Recommendation — replace, do not refine.**

This is not a taste judgement. Each failure below is a functional or positional defect with a stated consequence.

#### The five structural problems

**1. The script wordmark is the fatal flaw.**
"Bolamudi" is set in a formal calligraphic script with extreme thick/thin contrast and hairline connectors. Script wordmarks belong to a specific set of categories: wedding services, jewellery, bakeries, salons, catering, boutique hotels, and local trading houses. Not one infrastructure, energy or engineering firm on earth uses one — check Arup, Jacobs, WSP, Black & Veatch, Siemens Energy, ReNew, Adani Green, Tata Power. There is no exception because the association is too strong to overcome.

You explicitly briefed that the site must read as an established engineering consultancy and must not look like a startup. This wordmark solves the second problem by failing the first one harder: it doesn't look like a startup, it looks like a family retail business. To an IPP's business-development head or an infrastructure fund's diligence team, that is a worse first impression than looking new.

**2. Gold-on-dark-green is the wrong palette for this buyer.**
Metallic gold + deep green is one of the most heavily used combinations in Indian regional business branding — real estate, jewellery, construction contractors, political and community organisations, event and hospitality. It signals *prosperity and status*, which is a legitimate signal for those categories and the wrong one here. Your buyers are institutional. They are looking for *rigour and reliability*. Gold reads as self-presentation; the palette in Section 2 reads as ground truth.

**3. The mark carries five metaphors at once.**
Script "B" + gold crescent arc + commercial skyline + converging field furrows + a leaf on the "i". A logo can carry one idea. Five ideas means none of them lands, and the composition becomes decoration rather than a mark. Worse, two of them are actively wrong: the **skyline of commercial towers** implies real-estate or building construction, which is not the business; the **leaf** and the **rising arc** are the exact renewable-energy clichés the brief needs to avoid.

**4. It will not reproduce.**
The entire mark depends on gold-versus-green colour separation to be readable. Convert it to one colour — which is what happens on a government submission, a stamped annexure, a photocopied land document, a fax, an embroidered site jacket, or an etched sign — and the skyline merges into the "B", the furrows merge into each other, and the arc merges into the background. There is no single-colour version of this mark that retains its meaning. The metallic gradient compounds this: gradients cannot be reproduced in spot colour, embroidery, engraving or single-colour print at all.

**5. It reads as generated, not designed.**
The metallic gradient sweep, the stock-template composition, the mismatched icon strip, and the near-symmetrical arc are recognisable tells. Your audience has seen a lot of decks. A mark that looks template-derived undercuts the "20 years of execution" claim before a word of copy is read.

#### Secondary problems

- **The icon strip is not brand assets.** Five icons in a visual style unrelated to the mark: a literal government dome, a literal sun-and-panel, a growth arrow over a bar chart. The bar-chart-arrow is generic corporate clipart and "Value Creation" is not a service — it is a claim. These belong on a slide, not in an identity.
- **The service tagline is welded to the logo.** "LAND AGGREGATION | RENEWABLE ENERGY | INFRASTRUCTURE | GOVERNMENT LIAISON" should be a separate, editable descriptor element (see 1.3), not part of the artwork. As drawn, adding or changing a service line means redrawing the logo, and there is no Kannada version possible.
- **The leaf on the "i" dot** will disappear below roughly 200px and will fill in solid in print.

#### The one thing worth keeping

**The converging furrow lines.** That is a genuinely good idea and the only original element in the mark: perspective lines receding across land, reading simultaneously as ploughed fields, survey lines, and separate parcels converging toward a single point. It is land-specific, Karnataka-appropriate, not a renewable cliché, and nobody in the sector is using it. **Rebuild the mark around this device and discard everything else.**

#### If the mark cannot be replaced

If there is genuine attachment or sunk cost, the *minimum* intervention that makes this brand viable is not small, and you should understand that before choosing it:

1. Replace the script wordmark with Source Serif 4 SemiBold, letter-spacing −0.01em. **Non-negotiable — this alone is 70% of the problem.**
2. Delete the skyline, the arc, and the leaf. Keep the "B" (redrawn as a solid geometric form, not script) and the furrows.
3. Convert all gold to flat `ochre-600 #A9711A`. No gradients, ever.
4. Unweld the tagline; rebuild it as the endorsed lockup in 1.3.
5. Delete the icon strip entirely; rebuild those five services using Lucide icons per Section 10.

Steps 1–3 amount to a redraw of roughly 80% of the artwork. At that point you are paying for a new logo while keeping the constraints of the old one, which is the worst of both options. **My recommendation is a clean replacement per 1.2.**

### 1.1 The standard any Bolamudi logo must meet

Bolamudi's logo will spend most of its life in unglamorous places: the top-left of a 44px-tall mobile header, a black-and-white photocopy of a land agreement annexure, an email signature at 120px wide, a government submission cover page, and a 3m banner at a site inauguration. It will almost never be seen at the size it was designed at.

These are the seven tests applied in 1.0, and the acceptance criteria for any replacement. **Failing two or more means rework, not tidying.**

| # | Test | Pass condition | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Legibility at 24px height** | Wordmark still readable; symbol still distinguishable | Mobile header, favicon, WhatsApp profile. Over 70% of Indian B2B site traffic is mobile-first even for enterprise buyers. |
| 2 | **Single-colour reproduction** | Works as 100% #12100E on white and 100% white on dark, with no loss of meaning | Government forms, fax-quality scans, stamped documents, one-colour site signage. |
| 3 | **No gradients, no bevels, no drop shadows in the mark itself** | Flat vector only | Gradients date a mark to its decade and break in single-colour reproduction. Every reference firm you named (Arup, Jacobs, WSP, Black & Veatch) uses a flat mark. |
| 4 | **Aspect ratio between 1:1 and 4:1** | Fits both square (social avatar) and horizontal (header) lockups without a separate redraw | Ultra-wide marks force a bad avatar crop. |
| 5 | **Not a sun, not a leaf, not a wind turbine** | Symbol is not a literal renewable-energy cliché | Search "solar company logo" — the sun-ray-circle is used by thousands of firms. A cliché mark makes you look like a small EPC vendor, which is the exact positioning you said you must avoid. Bolamudi's business is **land and permissions**, not panels. |
| 6 | **Typeface of the wordmark is not a default system or trend font** | Not Poppins, Montserrat, Raleway, Bebas, Century Gothic | These read "made in Canva" to an infrastructure investor. |
| 7 | **Stroke weight survives 50% reduction** | Thinnest stroke ≥ 1/12 of the mark's cap height | Hairline marks vanish in print and embroidery. |

### 1.2 Replacement direction

Four concepts, in order of my recommendation. All are anti-cliché and land-derived, matching the positioning. All must be flat vector, single-colour-capable, and gradient-free.

> **Testing result — read before choosing.** Concept A was built and rendered at 24/48/128px. It failed: near-parallel bands read as generic corporate stripes; strong convergence read as trousers. Perspective furrows require a wide aspect ratio that cannot survive a favicon, and the gaps collapse below 1px at small sizes. **Concept C was built instead and passed every test. It is the delivered mark — see 1.2.1.**

**A. The Aggregated Field (built, failed testing).**
Three to five converging perspective lines forming a wedge, with the two outer parcels separated by a visible gap that closes toward the vanishing point. Meaning: *fragmented parcels resolved into one contiguous, connected site.* This is the company's core service stated as geometry.

Why this is the recommendation: it keeps the strongest element of the existing mark, so there is visual continuity rather than a hard break; it is unique in the sector; it reads as land, survey and agriculture simultaneously without a single cliché; and reduced to a 16px silhouette it is still a recognisable wedge. Executed in flat `green-700` with one parcel in `laterite-600`, it also carries the palette's regional argument. Draw it with hard-edged strokes of uniform weight, not tapering ribbons — tapering is what makes the current version fail test 7.

**B. The Survey Datum.**
A geometric mark built from a benchmark/survey reference symbol — a squared bracket or a corner tick with a centre point. Meaning: *the fixed point everything else is measured from.* This is exactly what Bolamudi is to a developer: the party that establishes ground truth on land, boundaries, and approvals. It is abstract, unique in the renewable sector, reproduces perfectly at 16px, and works as a repeatable graphic device (see 12.3, Corner Bracket).

**C. The Parcel Grid.**
Four to six irregular quadrilaterals fitted together — an abstraction of aggregated land parcels forming a single contiguous site. Meaning: *fragmentation resolved into one usable whole.* This is literally the company's core service. Risk: at very small sizes the irregular shapes can muddy; needs careful hinting.

**D. The Contour B.**
A monogram "B" constructed from stacked topographic contour lines. Meaning: terrain, survey, Karnataka's Deccan plateau. Risk: monogram marks are weaker at differentiating than symbolic marks, and horizontal-line constructions are fragile at small sizes. Recommended only if you need continuity with an existing "B"-based mark.

### 1.2.1 Delivered mark — "The Parcel Survey"

Four irregular quadrilaterals tiling a single rotated plot, separated by uniform 3-unit gaps. It is a cadastral map — the top-down view of aggregated land parcels, which is literally the document Bolamudi produces.

**Why it passed where the furrows failed:** compact near-square bbox (survives a 16px favicon), no tapering strokes (survives 50% reduction), gaps rather than colour carry the structure (survives single-colour), and it contains zero renewable-energy iconography.

**Known risk, stated plainly:** at 16–24px the four-quadrant arrangement has topological similarity to the Microsoft logo. Differentiators are the irregular non-orthogonal geometry, the plot rotation, the unequal parcel sizes, and a single dominant colour with one accent versus four equal squares in four colours. #### Deliverables to produce before launch

SVG (primary, mono-dark, mono-light, reversed) · horizontal lockup · stacked lockup · endorsed lockup · symbol-only · favicon.ico (16/32/48) · apple-touch-icon 180px · PNG @1x/2x/3x for each lockup · EPS + PDF vector for print and signage · one-colour outline version for embroidery and engraving.

### 1.3 Lockups (build all four regardless of which mark you use)

| Lockup | Composition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary — Horizontal** | Symbol · gap · "BOLAMUDI ENTERPRISE" | Site header, letterhead, email signature, proposal covers |
| **Secondary — Stacked** | Symbol above wordmark, centred | Square formats, site boards, sponsorship panels, social avatars |
| **Symbol only** | Symbol alone | Favicon, app icon, watermark, ≤32px contexts |
| **Endorsed** | Primary lockup + hairline rule + descriptor "Renewable Energy Project Development" | First-contact materials where the name alone doesn't explain the business |

The descriptor is set in Source Sans 3 SemiBold, uppercase, letter-spacing +0.08em, at **35% of the wordmark's cap height**. It is a separate text element, never merged into the logo artwork — so it can be translated to Kannada or dropped without redrawing the mark.

### 1.4 Clearspace and minimum size

- **Clearspace = X**, where X is the cap height of the letter "B" in the wordmark. No other element — text, image edge, page edge, rule — enters that zone on any side.
- **Minimum sizes:** horizontal lockup 120px wide (digital) / 32mm (print). Stacked lockup 72px wide / 20mm. Symbol alone 20px / 6mm.
- Below symbol minimum, use the wordmark set in type. Never scale below minimum "just this once" — that is how brands end up with an illegible favicon permanently.

### 1.5 Logo misuse — prohibited without exception

Stretching or condensing · rotating · recolouring outside the approved set · adding drop shadows, glows, strokes or 3D · placing on a busy photograph without a scrim (see 8.4) · enclosing in a shape not part of the approved lockups · using the symbol as a bullet or a decorative pattern element · animating the mark on page load · outlining the wordmark · placing on a background with less than 4.5:1 contrast against the mark.

## 2. Colour palette

### 2.1 The strategic argument

Almost every firm in this category defaults to corporate navy: Black & Veatch, Jacobs, WSP, AECOM. Navy is safe and therefore invisible. Bolamudi choosing navy would say "we are a smaller version of them."

You chose Karnataka-forward positioning, so the palette is derived from **the actual ground the company works on**:

- **Deccan Green** — the Western Ghats and the plateau scrub. Also the sector's own colour, but taken deep and desaturated so it reads as *land and permanence*, not as an eco-startup's mint green.
- **Laterite** — Karnataka's red lateritic soil, the literal material of the sites being aggregated. This is the single most regionally specific colour choice available, and no competitor is using it.
- **Granite** — the Deccan granite plateau. Used as a *warm-tinted* neutral scale (red/yellow undertone), not the cold blue-grey every SaaS product uses. This one decision does more to make the site feel earthy and Indian than any hero image will.
- **Sunlit Ochre** — restricted, small-dose accent for data and highlight only.

Deep green as the dominant colour, laterite as a rare accent, warm neutrals as the working surface. That combination is simultaneously serious, regional, and unoccupied in the competitive set.

| `laterite-700` | `#8A371E` | Accent text on light backgrounds (7.94:1, AAA) |
| `laterite-800` | `#6B2A17` | Text on laterite-100 |
| `laterite-900` | `#4A1D10` | Deepest accent |

**Hard rule — read this twice:** Laterite is **never** used for a primary call-to-action button, and **never** for a destructive/delete action. Two reasons, both non-negotiable:

1. Laterite `#A84528` and the system error red `#B42318` are close enough in hue that a user glancing at a form cannot reliably tell "submit" from "failed". Ambiguity in error signalling is an accessibility failure, not a taste preference.
2. Orange CTA buttons are a consumer-conversion pattern (Amazon, HubSpot). On a consultancy site they cheapen the page instantly.

Laterite's job is **editorial punctuation**: the 3px rule under a section label, the highlighted district on a Karnataka map, the underline on the active nav item, one number in a stat block. Roughly 3–5% of any given screen. If a page looks orange, it is broken.

**Why warm greys and not Tailwind `slate`/`gray`:** cool grey has a blue undertone that reads clinical and Silicon Valley. Warm grey shares an undertone with both laterite and the ochre in Karnataka landscape photography, so photographs sit on the page instead of floating on it. This scale is metrically close to Tailwind's `stone` ramp, which is deliberate — it means near-zero config drift and your developer gets sensible defaults for free.

**Why `granite-450` exists as an off-ramp value:** WCAG 2.2 requires 3:1 for non-text UI component boundaries. `granite-300` and `granite-400` both fail that. Rather than let a developer guess, the scale names the lightest legal interactive border explicitly. Verified: `#8F8A85` on white = 3.42:1.

### 2.5 Sunlit Ochre — restricted secondary

| Token | HEX | Contrast on white | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ochre-400` | `#E0A94E` | 1.9 | **Dark backgrounds only** (8.80:1 on green-950) |

### 2.6 Functional / semantic colours

| Role | HEX | On white | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Success | `#067647` | 5.69 AA | Must always pair with a check icon — it is close in hue to the brand green |
| Warning | `#A16207` | 4.92 AA | Always with a triangle-alert icon |
| Error | `#B42318` | 6.57 AA | Always with an icon and a text message; never colour alone |

Semantic colours are functional signals, not brand colours. They appear in forms, alerts and toasts. They must never be used decoratively — the moment error red appears as a design flourish, it stops meaning "error".

## 3. Typography

### 3.1 The pairing

| Role | Typeface | Weights | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Headings / display** | **Source Serif 4** | 400, 600, 700 | See below |
| **Body / UI** | **Source Sans 3** | 400, 600 | Metrically harmonised superfamily sibling — shared x-height and proportions mean serif and sans sit on the same baseline rhythm without manual correction |
| **Data / technical** | **IBM Plex Mono** | 400, 500 | Tabular figures for MW, acres, survey numbers, coordinates, dates, project codes |
| **Kannada** | **Noto Sans Kannada** | 400, 600 | The only Kannada face with reliable rendering across Android, iOS, Windows and print |

All four are open-source (SIL OFL), self-hostable via `next/font/local`. **Zero licensing cost, zero third-party request, zero layout shift.** For a firm dealing with government portals and slow rural connections, avoiding a Google Fonts round-trip is a real performance decision, not a purity one.

### 3.2 Why serif headings — the single most consequential decision here

Every renewable-energy startup uses a geometric sans (Poppins, Montserrat, Inter). Every large engineering consultancy uses a neo-grotesque (Helvetica, Söhne, Akzidenz). Bolamudi is neither, and imitating either makes it a lesser copy.

A serif does three things no sans can do here:

1. **It signals record and statute.** Bolamudi's product is documentary — land records, revenue survey numbers, gazette notifications, statutory approvals, ROW consents. Serif type is the visual language of legal instruments and official record. The typeface is arguing the same thing the copy is.
2. **It signals age.** You explicitly said the site must not look like a startup. A serif headline reads as an institution that predates the current funding cycle — appropriate for a founder with 20+ years of execution history.
3. **It differentiates instantly.** Put the site next to five competitors: it will be the only serif. In a category where everyone looks the same, typography is the cheapest differentiator available.

**Source Serif 4** specifically because it is a screen-first serif with low-contrast strokes and sturdy serifs — it holds up at 44px and at 22px, unlike a Didone (Playfair) which shatters at small sizes and reads as fashion, or a slab (Roboto Slab) which reads as blog.

**Honest counter-argument you should hear:** serif headings are marginally harder to read at very small sizes and can skew traditional to a younger audience. Mitigation: serif is used **only at 21px and above**. Below that, everything is Source Sans 3. If you later find the serif reading as dated rather than established, the fallback is Source Sans 3 SemiBold headings — a one-line token change, because the superfamily shares metrics.

### 3.3 Type scale — desktop (≥1024px)

Modular, 1.28 ratio, rounded to whole pixels for crisp rendering.

Tight leading looks efficient; here it looks cheap.

### 3.5 Typographic rules

- **Measure:** 62–75 characters per line for body. Above 80 the eye loses its return; below 45 the rhythm breaks. Enforce with `max-width: 68ch`.
- **Alignment:** left-aligned, ragged right. Never justify — CSS justification without hyphenation creates rivers of whitespace. Centre-align only for single-line section labels and short hero sub-heads.
- **Emphasis:** SemiBold (600) only. **Never italics for emphasis in UI** (poor screen rendering at small sizes); italics reserved for publication titles and Latin terms. Never underline anything that is not a link.
- **Numerals:** tabular lining figures everywhere numbers align in columns (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`). All statistics, capacity figures, tables and timelines use IBM Plex Mono.
- **Widows/orphans:** insert a non-breaking space before the last word of every heading. A single word alone on line two of an H1 makes a page look unedited.
- **All-caps:** permitted only for the `overline` token and button labels ≤3 words. Never for headings — all-caps destroys word-shape recognition and is measurably slower to read.
- **Kannada:** set at 1.06× the Latin size at the same optical position (Kannada glyphs read smaller at equal point size). Always wrap in `lang="kn"`.
- **Maximum three type sizes visible in any one component.** More than three and hierarchy stops being hierarchy.

## 4. Spacing system

### 4.1 Base unit: 4px, rhythm: 8px

4px is the smallest unit that survives every common device pixel ratio without sub-pixel blur. All spacing values are multiples of 4, and all *vertical* spacing between distinct blocks is a multiple of 8 — this keeps a consistent baseline rhythm without needing a strict baseline grid, which is impractical in responsive web.

| Token | px | rem | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `space-0` | 0 | 0 | Reset |
| `space-1` | 4 | 0.25 | Icon-to-label gap, badge padding |
| `space-2` | 8 | 0.5 | Tight inline gaps, chip padding |
| `space-3` | 12 | 0.75 | Input inner padding, small button padding |
| `space-4` | 16 | 1 | Default gap, card inner padding (mobile) |
| `space-5` | 20 | 1.25 | Paragraph spacing |
| `space-6` | 24 | 1.5 | Card inner padding, grid gutter |
| `space-8` | 32 | 2 | Card padding (desktop), between related blocks |
| `space-10` | 40 | 2.5 | Between sub-sections |
| `space-12` | 48 | 3 | Heading-to-content on section |
| `space-16` | 64 | 4 | Section padding (mobile) |
| `space-20` | 80 | 5 | Between major blocks |
| `space-24` | 96 | 6 | Section padding (tablet) |
| `space-32` | 128 | 8 | Section padding (desktop) |
| `space-40` | 160 | 10 | Hero padding, major section breaks |

### 4.2 Section vertical rhythm

| Breakpoint | Top/bottom section padding |
|---|---|
| <768px | 64px |
| 768–1023px | 96px |
| 1024–1439px | 128px |
| ≥1440px | 160px |

**Why so generous:** whitespace is the cheapest and most reliable signal of premium positioning. Compare Arup or Jacobs to a small EPC vendor's site — the difference is not better photography, it is that one page holds three ideas and the other holds twelve. Cramped layouts read as "we can't afford designers."

### 4.3 Proximity law

Space *within* a group must always be smaller than space *around* it. Concretely: card title → card body = 12px; card → card = 24px; card grid → next section = 128px. If these ever invert, the eye groups the wrong things and the page stops making sense before anyone reads a word.

## 5. Grid system

### 5.1 Breakpoints

| Name | Min width | Target |
|---|---|---|
| `xs` | 0 | Small phones (360–389) |
| `sm` | 390 | Standard phones |
| `md` | 768 | Tablet portrait |
| `lg` | 1024 | Tablet landscape / small laptop |
| `xl` | 1280 | Standard desktop — **primary design target** |
| `2xl` | 1536 | Large desktop |

Design at **1440px** first. That is the modal viewport for the corporate laptop your buyer actually uses. Designing at 1920 produces layouts that feel cramped on real machines.

### 5.2 Columns and gutters

| Breakpoint | Columns | Gutter | Outer margin | Max content width |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| xs–sm | 4 | 16px | 20px | fluid |
| md | 8 | 24px | 32px | fluid |
| lg | 12 | 24px | 48px | 960px |
| xl | 12 | 32px | 64px | 1200px |
| 2xl | 12 | 32px | auto (centred) | 1280px |

**Content max-width caps at 1280px.** Full-bleed backgrounds (dark sections, image bands) span the viewport; the *content inside them* stays within 1280px. Unbounded text on a 1920px monitor produces 140-character lines that nobody reads.

### 5.3 Standard layout patterns

| Pattern | Columns (xl) | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Full | 12 | Hero, image bands, full tables |
| Editorial | 7 content + 1 gap + 4 aside | Article pages, service detail with sticky contact card |
| Split | 6 + 6 | Text/image pairs, about sections |
| Asymmetric split | 5 + 7 | Text/image pairs — **preferred over 6+6** |
| Three-up | 4 + 4 + 4 | Service cards, insight cards |
| Four-up | 3 × 4 | Stat blocks, logo walls, capability grids |
| Offset text | 8 cols, starting col 3 | Long-form body copy |

**Why asymmetric 5+7 is preferred over 6+6:** a perfect 50/50 split creates visual stalemate — the eye has no entry point and the composition reads static. A 5+7 establishes hierarchy and movement while remaining formal. This is the single easiest change that makes a layout look designed rather than assembled.

### 5.4 Vertical grid discipline

Every section is a self-contained block: `padding-block` from the table in 4.2, content constrained to the container, no negative margins bleeding between sections. Sections alternate surface: `granite-50` → `white` → `granite-50` → `green-900` (dark punctuation) → `white`. **No more than one dark section per two screens of scroll** — dark sections are punctuation, and over-punctuated pages are exhausting.

## 6. Border radius

| Token | Value | Applied to |
|---|---|---|
| `radius-none` | 0 | Images, photography, tables, full-bleed panels, dividers, map tiles |
| `radius-xs` | 2px | Badges, tags, chips, table cells |
| `radius-sm` | 4px | **Buttons, inputs, selects, checkboxes** |
| `radius-md` | 6px | Cards, tooltips, dropdown panels |
| `radius-lg` | 8px | Modals, dialogs, large surfaces |
| `radius-full` | 9999px | Avatars and status dots **only** |

**Why the whole system is so square:** border radius is the most reliable single tell of brand age and category. 16–24px radii read as consumer SaaS and B2C app (Stripe, Linear, every 2021 startup). 0–6px reads as engineered, documentary, structural. Look at Arup, AECOM, Siemens Energy — near-square everything.

**Why not fully 0px:** pure zero on interactive elements reads as brutalist or as an unstyled form. 4px is the minimum that says "considered" without saying "friendly app." Photography stays at 0px because rounded photo corners are a consumer pattern and a rounded photo of a substation looks wrong.

**Never mix radii within one component.** A 6px card containing a 12px button is a visual error that people feel without identifying.

## 7. Shadows and elevation

### 7.1 Philosophy: borders first, shadows last

Heavy shadows are a consumer-app pattern. In an engineering context, definition should come from **structure** — a 1px border plus a surface tint — not from simulated depth. Shadows are reserved for elements that are genuinely floating above the page (dropdowns, modals, sticky headers on scroll). A card sitting in a grid is not floating; it gets a border, not a shadow.

All shadows use warm-tinted black `rgba(18, 16, 14, α)` — derived from `granite-950`, not pure `#000`. Pure black shadows against warm neutrals produce a grey, dirty halo.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `shadow-xs` | `0 1px 2px rgba(18,16,14,0.05)` | Subtle input depth on focus |
| `shadow-sm` | `0 1px 3px rgba(18,16,14,0.08), 0 1px 2px rgba(18,16,14,0.04)` | Card hover lift |
| `shadow-md` | `0 4px 8px -2px rgba(18,16,14,0.08), 0 2px 4px -2px rgba(18,16,14,0.04)` | Dropdowns, popovers |
| `shadow-lg` | `0 12px 24px -6px rgba(18,16,14,0.10), 0 4px 8px -4px rgba(18,16,14,0.05)` | Sticky header after scroll |
| `shadow-xl` | `0 24px 48px -12px rgba(18,16,14,0.14)` | Modals, dialogs |
| `shadow-inner` | `inset 0 1px 2px rgba(18,16,14,0.06)` | Pressed button state |

### 7.2 Elevation ladder

| Level | Treatment | z-index |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — Page | `granite-50`, no shadow | 0 |
| 1 — Surface | `white` + `1px granite-200` border | 0 |
| 2 — Raised | `white` + border + `shadow-sm` (hover only) | 10 |
| 3 — Floating | `white` + `shadow-md` | 100 |
| 4 — Sticky | `white` + `shadow-lg` + bottom border | 500 |
| 5 — Overlay | `shadow-xl` + `granite-950 @ 60%` backdrop | 1000 |

Never use two shadow levels on the same element, and never combine `shadow-lg` or above with a visible border — that reads as two competing depth systems.

## 8. Photography

### 8.1 Subject matter — what to shoot

Bolamudi's differentiator is **ground truth in Karnataka**. The photography has to prove presence, not describe an industry.

**Priority order:**

1. **Land and terrain, wide.** Karnataka scrubland, laterite soil, rocky Deccan plateau, dry-season fields — the land *before* it becomes a project. This is the single most under-used and most ownable image category in the sector, and it is literally what the company sells.
2. **Survey and fieldwork in progress.** Total station on a tripod, GPS unit, survey markers, drawings on a bonnet, boots on red soil. Human, specific, non-generic.
3. **Transmission and evacuation infrastructure.** Towers, lines, switchyards, substations against Karnataka sky. Proves the ROW capability.
4. **Meetings on site and in offices.** Real people, real documents, mid-conversation. Never staged, never eye-contact-with-camera.
5. **Built renewable assets, wide and contextual.** Solar farms and wind turbines shown *in landscape*, at distance, showing scale and land footprint — not glossy close-ups of a panel with a lens flare.

### 8.2 Banned imagery — non-negotiable

Stock handshakes · people in suits pointing at charts · 3D-rendered solar farms · glossy panel close-ups with sun flare · cupped hands holding a green sprout · Earth-from-space with glowing network lines · white male executives in a glass boardroom that is obviously not in India · anything with a visible Shutterstock/iStock aesthetic · AI-generated imagery of any kind.

**Why the ban is absolute:** your buyer is a developer or IPP who has visited a hundred sites. Generic stock imagery signals, unambiguously, that you have no projects of your own to show. One authentic mediocre photograph of an actual Karnataka site outperforms ten polished stock images. If a category has no real photo available yet, **use no photo** — use a map, a diagram, or a typographic panel instead.

### 8.3 Treatment and grading

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Saturation | −5 to −10% from camera default. Documentary, not tourism-board |
| Contrast | Slightly lifted blacks — no crushed shadows. Detail is credibility |
| White balance | Warm bias, 200–400K above neutral. Matches the granite/laterite palette |
| Grade | Preserve red-soil and dry-grass tones; do not push toward teal-and-orange |
| Filters | None. No duotone, no colour wash, no gradient map over photography |
| Format | AVIF primary, WebP fallback. Hero ≤200KB, inline ≤120KB |
| Corners | 0px radius, always |

### 8.4 Text on photography

Text is never placed directly on an unmodified photograph. Required scrim:

- **Linear scrim:** `linear-gradient(to right, rgba(7,22,15,0.88) 0%, rgba(7,22,15,0.65) 45%, rgba(7,22,15,0.15) 100%)` for left-aligned hero text
- **Flat scrim:** `rgba(7,22,15,0.62)` for centred text
- After scrim, the text must still verify at ≥4.5:1 against the **lightest pixel** it overlaps. Test the actual image, not the average.

The scrim uses `green-950 #07160F` rather than black — it ties the imagery back into the palette and keeps the warmth.

### 8.5 Aspect ratios

| Ratio | Use |
|---|---|
| 21:9 | Full-bleed hero bands |
| 16:9 | Section imagery, video thumbnails |
| 3:2 | Project cards, insight cards |
| 4:5 | Portraits (leadership) |
| 1:1 | Small thumbnails only |

Portraits: single subject, environmental (in an office or on site — never a grey studio backdrop), shot at eye level, shallow depth of field, warm grade matching 8.3.

## 9. Illustration and diagrams

### 9.1 The rule: no illustration of people, ever

No characters, no flat-vector figures with oversized heads, no isometric offices full of tiny workers, no hand-drawn doodle style. That entire visual language belongs to SaaS onboarding pages and would undo the positioning in a single scroll.

Bolamudi's illustration vocabulary is **technical drawing**, not decoration. It exists to explain, never to fill space.

### 9.2 Permitted illustration types

| Type | Purpose | Spec |
|---|---|---|
| **Karnataka district maps** | The signature brand asset — show operational districts, project locations, evacuation corridors | See 9.4 |
| **Process diagrams** | Land aggregation workflow, approval sequence, ROW process | Horizontal or vertical flow, numbered nodes, orthogonal connectors only |
| **Schematic line diagrams** | Site layout, evacuation single-line, transmission corridor | Technical-drawing convention: uniform stroke, no perspective |
| **Data visualisation** | Capacity, timelines, district coverage | See 9.5 |

### 9.3 Line-art specification

- Stroke: **1.5px** at 100% scale, uniform throughout. Never variable-width.
- Line colour: `granite-700 #44403C` primary; `granite-450 #8F8A85` for secondary/reference lines.
- Highlight: `laterite-600 #A84528` — **one highlight per diagram maximum.**
- Fills: none, or `granite-100 #F5F4F2` / `green-50 #F2F8F4` flat only. No gradients, no textures, no drop shadows.
- Corners: sharp 90° or a consistent 2px fillet. Never rounded blobs.
- Perspective: orthographic only. If isometric is unavoidable for a process diagram, use a single consistent 30° axis across every diagram on the site.
- Labels: Source Sans 3 SemiBold 13px, `granite-700`. Values in IBM Plex Mono.

### 9.4 Karnataka map treatment — the signature asset

This is the strongest identity device available and should appear on the homepage, the about page and every district-specific page.

- Base: Karnataka state outline, `granite-450 #8F8A85`, 1px stroke.
- District boundaries: `granite-300 #D6D3D1`, 0.75px, visible only at large display sizes.
- Inactive districts: `granite-100 #F5F4F2` fill.
- Active/operational districts: `green-700 #17422C` fill, white labels.
- Highlighted district (hover or focus of the narrative): `laterite-600 #A84528` fill, white labels.
- Project pins: 8px circles, `laterite-600`, with a 2px white ring for separation.
- Labels: Source Sans 3 SemiBold 12px, uppercase, +0.06em tracking.
- **Accessibility requirement:** the map is never the sole carrier of information. Every map is accompanied by a text list of the same districts. Colour-only encoding fails for colour-blind users and for anyone on a screen reader.

### 9.5 Data visualisation

**Categorical sequence (in strict order — do not reorder):**

1. `#17422C` green-700
2. `#A84528` laterite-600
3. `#78716C` granite-500
4. `#C98A2E` ochre-500
5. `#4E9670` green-400
6. `#D4794F` laterite-400

Six is the maximum. Beyond six, aggregate into "Other".

**Sequential ramp (single-variable intensity, e.g. capacity by district):**
`#E4EFE9` → `#C3DCCE` → `#8CBCA1` → `#2A7049` → `#17422C` → `#0C2418`

Axis labels in IBM Plex Mono 12px `granite-600`. Direct-label series where possible instead of a legend. Every chart has a caption stating source and date; unsourced numbers destroy credibility faster than an ugly chart does.

## 10. Icons

### 10.1 System

**Lucide React**, used exclusively. Not Heroicons, not Font Awesome, not Material, and never mixed. Mixed icon sets are the most common and most visible failure in otherwise good design systems — different grids, different stroke weights, different optical corrections, all instantly perceptible even to non-designers.

Lucide chosen because: 1500+ icons (enough that you will never need to source outside it), consistent 24px grid, stroke-based, MIT licensed, tree-shakeable in Next.js, and it has the infrastructure/industrial vocabulary this site needs (`map-pin`, `land-plot`, `route`, `file-check`, `zap`, `building-2`, `scale`, `ruler`, `layers`, `handshake`).

### 10.2 Specification

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Grid | 24 × 24 |
| Stroke width | **1.5px** at 24px |
| Stroke width at 32px+ | 1.5px (do not scale stroke proportionally) |
| Stroke width at 16px | 1.75px (optical compensation for small sizes) |
| Sizes | 16 / 20 / 24 / 32 / 48px only |
| Style | Outline only. **No filled, no duotone, no two-tone** |
| Default colour | `granite-700 #44403C` |
| On green surfaces | `green-300 #8CBCA1` or `white` |
| Accent/active | `laterite-600 #A84528` |
| Feature icons (48px) | `green-700 #17422C` |
| Disabled | `granite-400 #A8A29E` |

**Why 1.5px and not 2px:** 2px stroke at 24px reads slightly juvenile and app-like. 1.5px is finer and more technical, matching the 1.5px line-art in diagrams — icons and diagrams then look like one family rather than two.

### 10.3 Usage rules

- **Icons never replace labels in primary navigation or on buttons.** An icon-only nav is a usability failure for a 50-year-old executive. Icon + label, always, except for universally understood controls (close, menu, search).
- Feature icons sit at 48px in a 64px square with no background container — no circle, no rounded-square tile. Containers add visual noise and read as app-store iconography.
- Icons align optically, not mathematically. A triangle needs 1–2px nudging against a circle to look centred.
- **Never animate icons on scroll or hover** except for directional affordances (arrow shifting 4px on link hover) and toggle states (chevron rotating).
- Every decorative icon carries `aria-hidden="true"`. Every meaningful icon carries an accessible label.

## 11. Motion and animation

### 11.1 Principle: motion confirms, it does not perform

Motion in this brand exists to (a) confirm an interaction happened, (b) establish spatial relationship between states, (c) draw attention to content entering the viewport — once, subtly. It never exists to impress.

A landing page with parallax layers, scroll-jacking, animated gradients and counting numbers everywhere signals *startup marketing site*. A consultancy site earns trust through restraint. **If in doubt, animate less.**

### 11.2 Duration scale

| Token | Duration | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `duration-instant` | 100ms | Colour and background changes, active/pressed |
| `duration-fast` | 150ms | Hover states, focus rings, small toggles |
| `duration-base` | 200ms | Standard transitions, dropdowns |
| `duration-slow` | 300ms | Modals, drawers, accordion expand |
| `duration-reveal` | 500ms | Scroll-triggered content entrance |
| `duration-hero` | 700ms | Hero entrance on initial load only |

Nothing exceeds 700ms. Beyond that, animation stops feeling responsive and starts feeling like waiting.

### 11.3 Easing

| Token | Curve | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `ease-out-expo` | `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` | **Default for entrances.** Fast start, gentle settle — reads confident |
| `ease-standard` | `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)` | State changes, hovers, colour |
| `ease-in-out` | `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` | Elements exiting the screen |
| `linear` | `linear` | Progress bars and loading only |

**Never use spring/bounce easing.** Bounce is playful; playful is not the brand. Framer Motion's default spring must be explicitly overridden.

### 11.4 Approved patterns

| Pattern | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Scroll reveal** | `opacity 0→1`, `translateY 16px→0`, 500ms `ease-out-expo`, trigger at 15% viewport intersection, `once: true` |
| **Stagger** | 60ms delay per child, **maximum 6 children**. Beyond 6, animate the container as one block |
| **Hover — card** | `translateY -2px`, border `granite-200 → granite-450`, `shadow-sm`, 150ms |
| **Hover — button** | Background colour shift only, 150ms. No scaling, no lift |
| **Hover — link** | Underline grows left-to-right, 150ms; or arrow icon shifts `translateX 4px` |
| **Page transition** | Cross-fade 200ms. No slide, no wipe, no curtain |
| **Number counter** | Count up over 1200ms `ease-out-expo`, triggered once on entry. Statistics only |
| **Accordion** | Height auto-animate 300ms `ease-standard`, chevron rotates 180° in sync |
| **Sticky header** | On scroll >80px: shrink height 96→64px, add `shadow-lg`, 200ms |
| **Mobile drawer** | Slide from right 300ms `ease-out-expo`, backdrop fades 200ms |

### 11.5 Prohibited

Parallax backgrounds · scroll-jacking / scroll-hijacked section snapping · animated background gradients or mesh · particle fields · typewriter text effects · looping animations of any kind · logo animation on page load · `scale` transforms above 1.02 · horizontal scroll sections · cursor-following elements · text that animates in word-by-word or letter-by-letter · auto-playing video with sound.

### 11.6 Reduced motion — mandatory, not optional

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  *, *::before, *::after {
    animation-duration: 0.01ms !important;
    animation-iteration-count: 1 !important;
    transition-duration: 0.01ms !important;
    scroll-behavior: auto !important;
  }
}
```

Under reduced motion, scroll-reveal content must render **fully visible immediately** — never left at `opacity: 0`. This is the most common real-world accessibility bug in Framer Motion sites and it makes the page completely blank for the affected user. Test it before launch by enabling the OS setting.

## 12. Signature visual devices

These four devices, used consistently, are what make the site recognisable as Bolamudi rather than as a competent generic layout. Ownership comes from repetition, not from novelty.

### 12.1 The section label

Every major section opens with an overline: `laterite-600 #A84528`, 12px, SemiBold, uppercase, +0.12em tracking, preceded by a 24px × 2px laterite rule. Example: `— 01 / LAND AGGREGATION`. Numbered sequentially per page. This reads as document/report structure and reinforces the "engineering record" idea.

### 12.2 The hairline rule

1px `granite-200` horizontal rules separating content blocks, full container width. Used liberally. Rules are a documentary device — reports and drawings use them, marketing pages don't. This alone shifts the register meaningfully.

### 12.3 The corner bracket

A 24×24px open bracket (two 1.5px strokes forming an L) in `laterite-600`, placed at the top-left of key statistics and pull-quotes. Derived from survey/datum notation. Small, repeated, ownable.

### 12.4 The data strip

A full-width horizontal band, `green-900 #0C2418` background, containing 3–4 statistics in IBM Plex Mono `data-l` (white) with Source Sans 3 `caption` labels (`green-300 #8CBCA1`), separated by 1px `green-800` vertical rules. Used once per page maximum to state hard evidence: *20+ Years Execution · 12 Districts · 1,240 MW Facilitated · 8,400 Acres Aggregated*.

**Note:** replace those placeholder figures with real, defensible numbers before launch. Inflated or unverifiable statistics on an infrastructure site are a due-diligence liability, not a marketing win.

## 13. Buttons

### 13.1 Sizes

| Size | Height | Padding X | Font | Icon | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sm` | 36px | 16px | 14px / 600 | 16px | Table rows, compact UI |
| `md` | 44px | 24px | 15px / 600 | 18px | **Default** |
| `lg` | 52px | 32px | 16px / 600 | 20px | Hero CTA, form submit |

All sizes are ≥36px tall and every touch target has a ≥44×44px hit area (padding extended invisibly for `sm`). `radius-sm` 4px on all. Icon gap 8px. Label sentence case (`Request a consultation`), never ALL CAPS above 3 words, never Title Case.

### 13.2 Variants and states

**Primary** — the only button type used for the main action on any screen.

| State | Background | Text | Border |
|---|---|---|---|
| Default | `#17422C` | `#FFFFFF` (11.34:1) | none |
| Hover | `#1E5738` | `#FFFFFF` (8.48:1) | none |
| Active | `#123322` | `#FFFFFF` | none + `shadow-inner` |

**Secondary** — outline. Paired with primary for the alternative action.

| State | Background | Text | Border |
|---|---|---|---|
| Default | transparent | `#17422C` (11.34:1) | 1px `#8F8A85` (3.42:1 ✓) |
| Hover | `#F2F8F4` | `#17422C` | 1px `#17422C` |
| Active | `#E4EFE9` | `#123322` | 1px `#123322` |
| Disabled | transparent | `#A8A29E` | 1px `#E7E5E4` |

**Tertiary / Ghost** — low-emphasis inline actions.

| State | Background | Text |
|---|---|---|
| Default | transparent | `#44403C` (10.27:1) |
| Hover | `#F5F4F2` | `#17422C` |
| Active | `#E7E5E4` | `#123322` |

**Link button** — inline in prose.
`#17422C`, 1px underline at 2px offset, `underline-thickness: 1px`. Hover: `#1E5738`, thickness → 2px. Optional trailing `arrow-right` 16px shifting `translateX 4px` on hover.

**Destructive** — `#B42318` background, white text (6.57:1). Hover `#912018`. **Always paired with an icon and a confirmation step.**

**On dark surfaces** (`green-900`, `granite-950`): Primary inverts to `#FFFFFF` background with `#17422C` text. Secondary becomes 1px `#8CBCA1` border with `#FFFFFF` text.

### 13.3 Rules

- **One primary button per viewport.** Two primaries means neither is primary.
- Never place two buttons of the same variant adjacent — the pair is always Primary + Secondary.
- Buttons never use laterite (see 2.3), never use gradients, never scale on hover, never have a shadow at rest.
- Minimum 16px horizontal gap between adjacent buttons.
- Button width is content-driven; full-width only below 640px.

## 14. Cards

### 14.1 Base specification

All cards share: `white` background, 1px `granite-200 #E7E5E4` border, `radius-md` 6px, no shadow at rest, `space-6` (24px) padding on mobile and `space-8` (32px) on desktop. Hover (interactive cards only): `translateY(-2px)`, border → `granite-450 #8F8A85`, `shadow-sm`, 150ms `ease-standard`.

Where the entire card is a link, the anchor wraps the title and is expanded via a `::after` pseudo-element covering the card — this keeps a single, sensible tab stop and correct link text for screen readers.

### 14.2 Card types

**Service card** — for the six service lines.
48px Lucide icon (`green-700`), 20px gap, `h3` title (`granite-950`), 12px gap, `body-s` description (`granite-600`, max 3 lines), 24px gap, "Learn more →" link in `green-700`. Minimum height 280px so all six align in a 3-up grid regardless of copy length.

**Project / credential card**
3:2 image at 0px radius, flush to card edges (card padding does not apply to the image). Below: `overline` in `laterite-600` for the district, `h4` project name, `data` line in IBM Plex Mono for capacity and year, `body-s` one-line description.

**Statistic card**
Corner bracket (12.3) top-left, `data-l` figure in IBM Plex Mono `green-700`, `caption` label uppercase `granite-600` below. Background `granite-50`, no border. Used in 3-up or 4-up rows.

**Insight / article card**
16:9 image, `caption` date and category, `h4` headline (max 3 lines), `body-s` excerpt (2 lines), 4px reading-time meta in `granite-500`.

**Testimonial / client card**
No image. Large opening quotation set in Source Serif 4 `h3` weight 400 (not 600 — quotations read better at regular weight), attribution in `body-s` SemiBold plus `caption` role/company in `granite-600`. Left border 3px `laterite-600` — the one place laterite carries structural weight.

**Contact card (sticky aside)**
`green-900` background, white text, used in the Editorial layout. Contains a heading, one line of copy, and one inverted Primary button.

### 14.3 Rules

- Cards in a grid must be **equal height** (`items-stretch`), with the CTA pinned to the bottom via `margin-top: auto`. Ragged card bottoms are the clearest sign of an unmanaged layout.
- Maximum 3 cards per row at `xl`, 2 at `md`, 1 at `sm`. Four-up is reserved for statistic cards only.
- Never nest a card inside a card.
- Cards never carry a coloured background other than `white`, `granite-50`, or `green-900`.

| Property | Specification |
|---|---|
| Height | 48px (`md`), 56px (`lg`, used on the contact page) |
| Padding | 12px vertical, 16px horizontal |
| Background | `#FFFFFF` |
| Border | 1px `#8F8A85` granite-450 (3.42:1 ✓ — meets non-text minimum) |
| Radius | 4px |
| Text | `body` 17px, `granite-950 #12100E` |
| Placeholder | `granite-500 #78716C` (4.80:1) |
| Label | `body-s` 15px SemiBold `granite-700`, **always visible above the field** |
| Hover border | `granite-600 #57534E` |

Required fields are marked with the word "Required" in `caption` `granite-600` — not a red asterisk, which is unlabelled colour-and-symbol-only information.

## 16. Visual identity summary

### 16.1 The one-sentence brief

*Bolamudi Enterprise looks like a Karnataka land and permissions authority that happens to work in renewables — documentary, earth-derived, spacious, evidence-led, and completely uninterested in looking new.*

### 16.2 The five identity pillars

| Pillar | Expressed through |
|---|---|
| **Ground truth** | Laterite and granite palette, real site photography, Karnataka maps as signature asset |
| **Record and statute** | Serif headings, hairline rules, numbered section labels, IBM Plex Mono for every figure |
| **Permanence** | Deep green primary, 4–6px radii, minimal motion, generous whitespace |
| **Engineering precision** | 1.5px uniform strokes, 4px spacing grid, orthographic diagrams, tabular numerals |
| **Regional authority** | District maps, Kannada as first-class, place-specific photography, laterite red |

### 16.3 Tone of voice (governs copy, which governs layout)

- **Declarative, not promotional.** "We aggregated 8,400 acres across 12 districts," not "We are passionate about enabling India's clean energy transition."
- **Specific, always.** Districts, MW, acres, timelines, approval types. Vague copy on an infrastructure site reads as inexperience.
- **First person plural.** "We" — a firm, not a platform.
- **No superlatives without evidence.** "Leading," "world-class," "innovative," "cutting-edge," "one-stop solution" are banned. Every one of them is a claim your buyer will discount instantly.
- **Sentence case for all headings.** Title Case reads American-corporate; ALL CAPS reads shouty.
- **Indian English conventions:** lakh/crore where the audience expects them, DD Month YYYY dates, ₹ with the Indian digit grouping (₹1,00,000).

### 16.4 The competitive-distance test

Screenshot the homepage next to Arup, Black & Veatch, and three Indian renewable-services competitors, then desaturate all six to greyscale. Bolamudi should be identifiable by **layout rhythm and typographic register alone**. If it is only identifiable by its logo, the identity has not been applied hard enough.

## 17. Brand consistency rules

### 17.1 Never

1. Never introduce a colour outside this document. If something needs a new colour, the system is wrong — fix the system, don't add a one-off hex.
2. Never use laterite for a CTA or a destructive action (2.3).
3. Never use ochre for body text (2.5).
4. Never use a typeface outside the four specified (3.1).
5. Never mix icon libraries (10.1).
6. Never use a border radius above 8px on any element except avatars.
7. Never use a gradient anywhere — backgrounds, buttons, text, or the logo. The only permitted gradient is the photographic scrim in 8.4.
8. Never use stock imagery of the banned categories (8.2).
9. Never illustrate people (9.1).
10. Never place more than one primary button in a viewport (13.3).
11. Never rely on colour alone to convey information (18.4).
12. Never use emoji, exclamation marks in headings, or ALL-CAPS headings.
13. Never animate the logo.
14. Never ship a page whose reduced-motion state hides content (11.6).

### 17.2 Always

1. Always run new colour pairings through a contrast checker before shipping — 4.5:1 for body, 3:1 for large text and UI boundaries.
2. Always give every image meaningful alt text describing what it shows, including the district where relevant.
3. Always pair semantic colour with an icon and a text label.
4. Always keep body text at 16px minimum on mobile.
5. Always cap content width at 1280px and text measure at 68ch.
6. Always test at 390px, 768px, 1024px, 1440px, and at 200% browser zoom.
7. Always use design tokens in code — never a raw hex in a component.
8. Always source and date every statistic shown.

### 17.3 Governance

- **Single source of truth:** these tokens live in `tailwind.config.ts` and a CSS custom-property layer. A hard-coded hex in a component is a defect and should fail code review.
- **Change process:** any addition to the palette, type scale or component set is a version bump to this document with a written rationale. Undocumented additions are how design systems rot.
- **Quarterly audit:** grep the codebase for raw hex values, non-token spacing values, and `!important`. Each occurrence is technical debt in the brand, not just in the code.

## 18. Accessibility

Target: **WCAG 2.2 Level AA**, with AAA on body text where the palette already achieves it. This is not optional decoration — Indian government and PSU procurement increasingly references accessibility, and your buyers include institutional investors with their own ESG and accessibility diligence.

### 18.1 Colour and contrast

- Body text ≥4.5:1. The default pairing `granite-700 #44403C` on `granite-50 #FAF9F7` = **9.76:1**, comfortably AAA.
- Large text (≥24px, or ≥19px bold) ≥3:1.
- UI component boundaries and graphical objects ≥3:1. This is why `granite-450 #8F8A85` exists and why `granite-300` and `granite-400` are barred from functional borders.
- All verified pairings are listed in 2.7. **Do not use a pairing that is not on that list without re-verifying it.**

### 18.2 Focus visibility (WCAG 2.2 — 2.4.11)

Every interactive element has a visible focus indicator: **2px solid `green-500 #2A7049` with a 2px offset**, using `outline` (not `box-shadow`, which disappears in Windows High Contrast Mode). On dark surfaces the ring becomes `green-300 #8CBCA1`. On green-700 buttons, a 2px white inner ring separates the indicator from the fill.

`outline: none` without a replacement indicator is a build-blocking defect.

### 18.3 Targets and input (WCAG 2.2 — 2.5.8)

- Minimum touch/click target 44 × 44px, including invisible padding.
- Minimum 8px gap between adjacent targets.
- All functionality reachable by keyboard alone, in a logical DOM order.
- A "Skip to main content" link, visually hidden until focused, is the first focusable element on every page.
- No functionality requires hover or a pointer-specific gesture.

### 18.4 Not colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1)

Every piece of information carried by colour is duplicated in text, icon, or pattern:

| Context | Colour | Redundant cue |
|---|---|---|
| Form error | Red border | Alert icon + text message |
| Form success | Green border | Check icon |
| Active nav item | Laterite underline | `aria-current="page"` + SemiBold weight |
| Map: operational district | Green fill | Text list of districts alongside the map |
| Chart series | Palette order | Direct labels or pattern fills |
| Required field | — | The word "Required", not a red asterisk |

### 18.5 Structure, semantics and language

- One `<h1>` per page; heading levels never skip.
- Landmarks: `<header>`, `<nav>`, `<main>`, `<aside>`, `<footer>`. All content inside a landmark.
- Every `<img>` has `alt`; decorative images get `alt=""`. Diagrams and maps get a text alternative describing the data, not the picture.
- `<html lang="en-IN">`; Kannada content wrapped in `lang="kn"` so screen readers switch voice.
- Forms use `<label for>` — never `aria-label` as a substitute for a visible label.
- Tables use `<th scope>` and `<caption>`.
- Live regions (`aria-live="polite"`) for form submission results.

### 18.6 Motion, media and zoom

- `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured globally (11.6), with content visible in the reduced state.
- No content flashes more than 3 times per second.
- Video: captions required, no autoplay with sound, visible pause control.
- Layout functional at **200% browser zoom** and at 320px CSS width with no horizontal scroll and no loss of content (WCAG 1.4.10 Reflow).
- Text spacing overrides (line-height 1.5×, paragraph spacing 2×, letter spacing 0.12em) must not clip content (WCAG 1.4.12) — this means no fixed-height text containers.

### 18.7 Pre-launch verification

| Check | Tool / method |
|---|---|
| Automated audit | axe DevTools + Lighthouse, 0 critical issues |
| Keyboard walkthrough | Every page, tab-only, no mouse |
| Screen reader | NVDA on Windows Chrome, VoiceOver on Safari |
| Contrast | Every unique pairing verified; results appended to 2.7 |
| Reduced motion | OS setting on — confirm no blank sections |
| Zoom | 200% and 400%, 320px width |
| Colour blindness | Deuteranopia and protanopia simulation, especially on maps and charts |
| Real device | Mid-range Android on 4G — the actual conditions of a site visit |

## 19. Implementation reference — token naming

```
Colour     color-{family}-{step}      color-green-700, color-laterite-600
Spacing    space-{n}                  space-6  (= 24px)
Type       text-{token}               text-h2, text-body, text-data-l
Radius     radius-{size}              radius-sm
Shadow     shadow-{size}              shadow-md
Motion     duration-{speed}           duration-base
Easing     ease-{name}                ease-out-expo
Z-index    z-{layer}                  z-sticky, z-overlay
```

Semantic aliases layered on top of primitives, so a future palette change is a one-line edit:

```
bg-surface          → white
bg-page             → granite-50
bg-subtle           → granite-100
bg-inverse          → green-900
text-primary        → granite-950
text-secondary      → granite-700
text-tertiary       → granite-600
text-muted          → granite-500
text-brand          → green-700
text-accent         → laterite-700
text-on-inverse     → white
border-subtle       → granite-200
border-default      → granite-450
border-strong       → granite-600
border-brand        → green-700
action-primary      → green-700
action-primary-hover→ green-600
focus-ring          → green-500
```

Components always consume the semantic layer, never the primitive layer.
