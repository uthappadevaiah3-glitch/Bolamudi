01 — The Position
We occupy the stage most developers underestimate.
A utility-scale renewable project rarely fails on technology. It fails, or slips by eighteen months, in the pre-construction stage — where land title is imperfect, where a statutory file sits unmoved on a desk, where an evacuation approval is contingent on a bay already allocated, where a corridor crosses forty parcels and thirty-nine owners agree.
That stage is our entire practice. We do not build, and we do not advise on what to build. We take a project from allocation to a construction-ready position.
Not an EPC contractor
We do not construct or procure.
Not a technical or DPR consultant
We do not size plants, model yield or prepare engineering reports.
Not a land broker
Retained by the developer. Not paid by the landowner. No positions held in land we aggregate.
The third exclusion is the most commercially valuable line on the page. Conflict of interest is the number one unspoken concern about an aggregation partner in India. Answering it unprompted removes an objection from a meeting you may never get.

Development support is a term wide enough to mean nothing. Below is what it means here — the specific instruments, authorities and procedures we work through in Karnataka.

Crop and structure compensation assessment and settlement
Objection handling and district administration escalation

Founder & Principal

On why this practice exists. Twenty years of executing utility-scale solar, wind, hybrid and pumped storage projects in Karnataka taught me one thing repeatedly: the difference between a project that commissions on schedule and one that slips two years is almost never engineering. It is land, statute and relationships, handled early and handled properly.

On how the work is done. Development support is not a document exercise. It is field work — village visits, survey records, taluk offices, individual landowners, and the same officials over years rather than weeks. It cannot be delegated to someone learning it on your project. Every engagement here is led by me personally.

On what I will not do. I will not commit to an aggregation timeline before title and owner consent have been tested on the ground. I will not report a statutory position I have not confirmed at source. A developer told the truth late has lost a year; a developer told the truth early has lost a week.

05 — Core Values
Operating commitments.
01
Verified before reported.
No status reaches a client that has not been confirmed at source. We do not relay assurances we have not tested.
02
Land first, promises second.
No aggregation timeline is committed until title and owner consent have been tested in the field. Optimistic land schedules are the most expensive error in Indian renewable development.
03
The landowner is a stakeholder, not an obstacle.
Fair, transparent, documented dealing at village level. Every acre acquired badly becomes a litigation, an agitation or a stalled tower three years later — and it becomes the developer's problem, not the aggregator's.
04
Compliance over speed.
We will not take a route that clears a file faster and leaves a title or statutory defect behind. Defects surface at diligence, refinancing or sale, when they cost the most.
05
One principal, one accountable owner.
The person who assessed your site is the person in the taluk office and the person on your call. No handoffs, no account-management layer.
Numbered editorial list, no icons, no card grid. Icon-and-card value grids are the most templated pattern on the web. Value 03 is the strategic centrepiece — it frames landowner fairness as the developer's risk mitigation, not as CSR. Value 04 names diligence, refinancing and sale: written for the investor in the room, not the engineer.
06 — Why Bolamudi
Three honest reasons, and one honest answer.
Against a large consultancy
Large firms sell with partners and deliver with associates. On development support the value is entirely in the relationships and field knowledge of the person doing it. Here, the person who sells the work is the person who does the work.
Against an in-house team
An internal development team is built for a pipeline, not a project. For a developer entering Karnataka, twenty years of district-level relationships cannot be hired in a quarter and cannot be justified as permanent overhead.
Against a local aggregator
A land aggregator solves land and stops. Land solved in isolation from evacuation, statutory sequence and right of way produces a site you own and cannot build on. The stages are interdependent and are handled here as one scope.
On continuity and single-principal risk
The reasonable question about a principal-led practice is what happens if the principal is unavailable or already committed. We answer it directly rather than leave it unasked.
Engagements are accepted against declared capacity — if a project cannot be given proper attention in the window you need, we say so before contracting rather than after. Field execution is supported by an established network of district-level associates who have worked with us for years, under our direction and accountability. Every engagement maintains a documented file — title chain, application status, authority correspondence, contact map — held with the client, so project knowledge sits with your organisation and not only in our heads.
Name the objection before the reader does. Every buyer thinks "what if this one person is unavailable" within ten seconds. Hiding from it doesn't remove the thought — it removes your chance to answer. Three mechanisms offered, not reassurance. Client-held documentation directly answers the investor's key-man concern. Testimonial slot goes directly beneath this section when you have one.
